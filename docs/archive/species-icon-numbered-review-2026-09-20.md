# Numbered species icons — review of 20 September 2026

Dated review of the current numbered PNG masters, distinct from the earlier 62-file review on the same date. Historical screening, not an independent mycological sign-off or a record of corrections applied.

## Scope and result

All **61 files representing 60 species** were visually inspected on seven labelled contact sheets (410-pixel image frames). Six cases were additionally viewed individually: 06, 11, 18, 21, 40 and 45. There are two Tricholoma terreum versions; sequence numbers 05 and 07 are absent. The previous review includes species absent from this folder and must not be treated as its current inventory. Artwork and generation prompts were left unchanged; prompts were not evidence of anatomy.

**4 corrections, 9 refinements, 48 screened without a conspicuous mismatch.** Screened means no obvious contradiction at this viewing scale, not complete validation. Microscopic characters, smell, texture, size without scale, and biological variation cannot be established from icons. Sources were checked for disputed features; every species was not independently researched.

## Secondary refinements

- **20-Craterellus_tubaeformis.png**: Use greyer/beige mature underside folds and clearer branching/cross-veins to distinguish it from lutescens. Yellowish undersides occur, so colour alone is not an error. [Reference](https://www.mushroomexpert.com/craterellus_tubaeformis.html).
- **21-Hygrophoropsis_aurantiaca.png**: Forking is now visible, but blade edges remain thick and the silhouette very chanterelle-like. Make true gills thinner and more crowded; retain forked orange blades and darker stem base. [Reference](https://explore.beatymuseum.ubc.ca/mushroomsup/H_aurantiaca.html).
- **24-Tricholoma_terreum_v1.png**: Both versions have very smooth, sharply pointed caps. A more finely felted/scaly surface and less exaggerated adult peak would be more representative. Neither version clearly resolves this; v2 is only a modest visual preference. Visual refinement suggestion; not independently source-validated in this review.
- **24-Tricholoma_terreum_v2.png**: Both versions have very smooth, sharply pointed caps. A more finely felted/scaly surface and less exaggerated adult peak would be more representative. Neither version clearly resolves this; v2 is only a modest visual preference. Visual refinement suggestion; not independently source-validated in this review.
- **38-Rubroboletus_satanas.png**: Pale cap, red pores and swollen red/yellow stem are clear. Fine red stem reticulation is not visible; add it if the icon will be enlarged. Visual refinement suggestion; not independently source-validated in this review.
- **40-Calocybe_gambosa.png**: Gill density is improved, but the notched attachment is unclear, especially on the tilted specimen. Clarify the notch and narrow close blades. [Reference](https://micologiaalbarracin.es/calocybe-gambosa/).
- **46-Paxillus_involutus.png**: Inrolled brown margin reads well. Underside remains a few thick descending ribs; make crowded thin true gills, with branching near the stem. [Reference](https://www.aranzadi.eus/index.php/buscador-micologico/ficha/1-1-002.03.01.00.01.00).
- **61-Tuber_melanosporum.png**: Dark warty exterior and pale internal marbling read correctly at icon scale. Finer more numerous branching veins would improve an enlarged educational depiction. Visual refinement suggestion; not independently source-validated in this review.
- **62-Craterellus_cornucopioides.png**: Dark hollow trumpet reads correctly. Soften the long high-contrast outer ribs into low irregular wrinkles so they do not suggest true gills. Visual refinement suggestion; not independently source-validated in this review.

## Useful features now present

The current Hydnum repandum shows teeth over the underside; Russula virescens has an angular cracked cap pattern; Suillus granulatus has pale droplets and a ringless dotted stem. The Lactarius deliciosus and sanguifluus pair now has numerous narrow blades and distinct orange versus wine-red latex. Lactarius torminosus has fibrous rather than scalloped edging. Morchella has connected honeycomb ridges, and both Ramaria icons have repeated branching. These visible improvements mean the earlier correction list cannot simply be carried forward.

Amanita caesarea retains yellow gills/stem/ring and a white volva; phalloides has an olive cap with white gills/ring/volva. Muscaria and pantherina retain their distinctive cap colours and pale warts. Boletes retain the main cap-colour, pore-surface and stem-pattern contrasts. These are useful illustrative cues, not sufficient identification criteria.

Both terreum files remain plausible stylizations; v2 has a slightly cleaner silhouette, but does not materially improve the cap texture. Choose between them on layout only after that feature is addressed.
